## Air/atmospheric pollution

### 1. Tropospheric pollution 

Humans require ~12-15 x more air than food.

1. Troposphere: 0-10 km MSL. most aerial and particulate and vapour related movements/interactions. clouds,dust and turbulence.
2. Stratosphere: 10-50 km MSL. ozone layer. calm(er), dioxygen, dinitrogen, ozone, water vapour. ozone layer blocks 99.5% incoming UV.

- $SO_2$:
	- Produced when S containing fuels are burnt, in volcanic eruptions
	- Asthma, Bronchitis, Emphysema
	- Eye irritation
	- Stiffening of flower buds
	- Uncatalysed oxdn is slow; particles/ozone/hydrogen peroxide in the air accelerate the reaction : $\ce{SO2 + O2 -> SO3}$ 
- N oxides:
	- Formed at high altitudes $\because$ lightning 
	- $NO$ and $NO_2$:
		- $\ce{N2 + O2 ->[1483K/1210\degree C] NO}$ 
		- $\ce{NO + O2 ->["instantly"] NO2}$
		- $\ce{NO + O3 ->[even faster] NO2 + O2}$
		- Irritant red haze in congested places (Marthahalli traffic jam)
		- Leaf damage, poison for photosynthetic activities
		- Lung irritant, causes acute respiratory diseases, toxic to living tissues
		- Harmful to textile fibres and metals
- Hydrocarbons:
	- Formed (as a pollutant) by incomplete combustion
	- Carcinogenic
	- Cause ageing, breakdown of tissues and shedding of leaves, flowers and twigs
- C oxides:
	- $CO$
		- produced by incomplete combustion (mainly automobile exhaust)
		- colourless, odourless, burns with blue flame
		- highly poisonous due to excellent coordinating capacity with haemoglobin (300 x more than dioxygen) due to synergic/back-$\pi$ bonding
		- toxic \[CO---Hb] complex concentration limit = 3-4%; excess can cause suffocation, headache/nausea, weak eyesight, cardiovascular disorders
		- smoking (any among cigarettes, cigars, beedis, Connecticuts, cigarillos, Churchills,  pipes) can release CO
	- $CO_2$
		- produced by respiration, complete combustion, volcanic eruptions
		- found only in troposphere; comprises ~0.03% v/v of atmosphere
		- excess $CO_2$ is removed by photosynthesis (maintaining oxygen-carbon dioxide balance)
		- causes global warming

##### Global Warming
~75% of solar energy is directly absorbed by Superficies Terrae, rest is reflected back.

Gases responsible: $\ce{CO2, CH4, O3, CFCs, H2O (vap), NO}$ 
- Methane: organic matter burnt/digested/rotted in less oxygen
- CFC: produced by conventional cooling mechanisms 
- NO: occurs naturally
Melting of ice caps => flooding (because more water water); dengue, malaria, yellow fever, sleeping sickness

##### Acid Rain
pH of normal rainwater = 5.6 due to carbonic acid formation
pH of acid rainwater < 5.6, if not already obvious
pH is brought down by oxides of N and S in the air forming sulfuric/ous and nitric/ous acids. Particulate matter in the air catalyses these reactions.
Ammonium salts are also formed: seen as aerosol haze; wet deposition.
SO2: both dry and wet deposition.
Of course, harmful etc. 
Taj Mahal dying due to this (major component: basic $\ce{CaCO3}$ in marble form)

##### Particulate Pollution
_Caused by minute solid particles or liquid droplets in the air_ (very important definition)
Vehicle emissions, smoke, dust, ash
1. Viable: basically, living. bacteria, fungi, moulds, algae. mostly allergens.
2. Non-viable: 
	1. Smoke: cigarette, cigar, beedi, Connecticut, cigarillo, Churchill,  pipe. fire/burning,/combustion. mixture of solid and liquid particles.
	2. Dust: fine solid particles >1µm diameter. produced by crushing, grinding, sand, sawing, dust storms; fly ash, sawdust, sand etc.
	3. Mist: liquid/aerosol formed by aerial vapour condensation. sulfuric acid mist, herbicides, insecticides.
	4. Fumes:  vapour condensation during sublimation/distillation/boiling. generally consists of solvents, metals, metal oxides.
Size matters, at least here, which decides the polluting effects of particles.
_Dangerous for human health._ Max _not dangerous_ particle size = ~5µm (larger ones get stuck in the nasal cavity/passages). Particles of ~10µm can enter the lungs easily (pneumonoultramicroscopicsilicovolcanoconiosis).
Airborne particulate lead: ex-major pollutant, mainly produced by combustion of petrol infused with tetra ethyl lead (TEL) as an anti-knocking agent, which is now replaced by ethanol and BTEX etc. Lead interferes with the development and maturation of RBCs.

##### Smog
=smoke+fog (संधिविच्छेदः)
Most common type of pollution (since two (pollutions) are better (here, worse) than one (pollution)).
1. Classical smog: cool humid climate. Mixture of (smoke, fog, and) sulfur dioxide, therefore is reducing, therefore is also called 'reducing smog'.
2. Photochemical smog: warm dry sunny climate. Formed by action of sunlight on olefins, acetylenes and N oxides, therefore is oxidising, therefore is also called 'oxidising smog'.
	- Formed by a chain reaction initiated by light
	- $\ce{NO + O2 ->[heat/sunlight] NO2}$
	  $\ce{NO2 ->[hν][photolysis] NO + [O]}$ (free oxygen is produced)
	  $\ce{[O] + O2 <=> O3}$
	  $\ce{O3 + NO -> NO2 + O2}$ 
	- All products in the above reaction spare dioxygen, contribute to haze or are pollutants or are toxic.
	- $NO$ and $NO_2$ can react with aerial hydrocarbons to produce formaldehyde, acrolein and peroxyacetylnitrate (PAN).
	- Ozone and PAN formed during the reactions are nose and throat irritants: can cause headaches, chest pain, throat dryness, cough, breathing difficulty.
	- The smog itself can cause cracking of rubber, damage to plant life, corrosion of metals, stones, rubber, paints.
	- Can be prevented by controlling emissions of $NO$, $NO_2$ and hydrocarbons, and curbing remnant Ozone and PAN. Catalytic converters used in automobiles achieve the former.  Pinus (Pine), Juniparus (Juniper (not Grover's girlfriend)), Quercus (Oak), Pyrus (Pear) and Vitis (Grapevine) can metabolise NO to further aid in reducing photochemical smog.

### 2. Stratospheric pollution

##### Ozone breakdown
Peak UV λ = 255nm : cause melanoma

In the stratosphere
$\ce{O2 ->[UV] 2[O]}$
$\ce{O2 + [O] <=>[UV] O3}$ (10% $O_3$ mix, generally)

Freons: nonreactive, non flammable, non toxic. composed of Cl, F and C (the name, Chlorofluorocarbons hence). The $\dot{\text{Cl}}$ released on the decomposition of a freon molecule by UV radiation reacts with ozone: 
$\ce{\dot{\text{Cl}} + O3 -> Cl\dot{\text{O}} + O2}$  
and further
$\ce{Cl\dot{\text{O}} + [O] \text{ (from UV aided breakdown of} O2) -> \dot{\text{Cl}} + O2}$

## Water pollution

Mostly not natural - humans contribute to most of water pollution.

1. Point Source: easily identifiable source of pollution - say discharge pipes
2. Non-point Source: say agricultural run off, acid rain, rainwater/storm-water drainage
